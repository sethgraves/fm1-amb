# DREAM manual

DREAM holds pure sine tones in just intonation: every tone a whole-number harmonic of one frequency, locked in
phase, with nothing added. Switch the FM-1 on and the first piece swells in under a magenta glow. Touch any control
to go straight to the screen.

These pieces were made to sound for hours or days in a room. The few recordings that were released are long out of print, and you can't buy them new today. DREAM lets you keep a version of that sound going at home, for as long as it was meant to last.

## Playing

- **PLAY** starts and stops the drone. Tones swell in and fade out over a few seconds.
- **PRESET** picks the piece; **ALGORITHM** picks its version (the Dream House's bases, the Magic Opening Chord,
  Composition 1960 #7's octave). Its name shows above the tones.
- **MASTER** is the volume. Pieces with many tones play quieter so they can never clip: turn it up.
- **SAVE** keeps everything, piece and version included, for the next power-on.

## The knobs

Two pages of four. Turn **SELECT** clockwise for the second page, counterclockwise for the first; the two dots at
the bottom right show which.

| Page 1 | |
| --- | --- |
| PITCH | the whole series up or down, in semitones. The tones glide together and keep their ratios |
| FINE | the same, in cents |
| SPREAD | the tones across the stereo field, neighbours on opposite sides |
| TILT | the levels sloped up the series, in dB per octave |

| Page 2 | |
| --- | --- |
| DENSITY | how many tones sound. The simplest harmonics stay longest (in the Dream House, 224 and 288 last) |
| BALANCE | the base tones against the rest, in dB |
| DRIFT | 0 holds every tone phase-locked. Up, each tone moves a fraction of a hertz (at most 0.1 Hz), too little to hear as pitch, so the shape of the combined wave slowly changes |
| EVOLVE | the density breathes on its own, down to one tone and back, once in the minutes you set. OFF holds it |

The second page starts over with each new piece: DENSITY 100%, BALANCE 0, EVOLVE off, DRIFT the piece's own (40
for the drift study).

## The screen

- Two tones: their ratio, large, and each tone's harmonic, note, cents from equal temperament and frequency.
- Three to eight: the ratio in lowest terms, and the tones in two columns with their levels.
- More: the tones as lines placed by pitch, violet low to magenta high, each as tall as its level; the base
  underneath.

The note names are the nearest equal-tempered notes; the cents say how far each tone sits from them. The
Well-Tuned Piano's Eb is about a quarter-tone flat of a standard Eb, so it reads as D +25C. CPU is the share of the
FM-1's time the sound takes.

## The pieces

| Piece | |
| --- | --- |
| The Opening Chord | The Well-Tuned Piano's, 4:6:7:8:9:12 on Young's Eb (297.99 Hz) |
| Well-Tuned Piano Tuning | all twelve of its pitches in three octaves, 36 tones. Good with DENSITY and EVOLVE |
| Map of 49's Dream | a 60 Hz drone under 49:56:63:72, without the number 5. Chosen by Young's rules; his own sets are unpublished |
| Drift Study | three tones, 6:7:8 on B, drifting. The recordings' own tones are unpublished: these are chosen |
| The Magic Chord | The Well-Tuned Piano's: eight tones over an Eb that never sounds; or the Magic Opening Chord, both chords together |
| Dream House | the 15 tones from harmonic 224 to 288 of *The Base 9:7:4 Symmetry in Prime Time…*, worked from its title, over any of its eight optional bases |
| Composition 1960 #7 | B and F#, "to be held for a long time", as a pure fifth (3:2). In B2, B3 or B4 |
